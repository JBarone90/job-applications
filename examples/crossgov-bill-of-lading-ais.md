## People management

Supporting and motivating a team to deliver excellence and derive fulfilment from their roles.

I led the technical supervision of 8 analysts and data scientists across three government organisations (DBT, DfT, ONS) to deliver an experimental publication linking UK Bill of Lading data with AIS vessel-tracking information. While each analyst worked within their departmental structure, I coordinated technical goals and provided support across this distributed team.

My approach centred on clarity, engagement, and trust. I chaired weekly coordination meetings with documented actions to prevent duplication, organised via Kanban boards. I also established separate technical sessions for deeper methodological discussions. This ensured policy colleagues stayed informed without being overwhelmed, while allowing analysts space for complex brainstorming on technical challenges.

I provided hands-on support through code reviews on GitLab. I also proactively sought regular one-to-one contact with team members to discuss perspectives and concerns that don't always emerge in group meetings, helping individuals express themselves more openly.

Trust became crucial when data quality issues emerged. I produced clear analytical summaries and presented them to the extended team, enabling us to make informed decisions together, even when this meant excluding certain key trade routes. To recognise the team's exceptional effort, I nominated our work for the Analysis in Government Awards, ensuring their contributions gained visibility across government and demonstrating my appreciation for their dedication.

We successfully delivered the world's first public sector Bill of Lading-AIS linkage on schedule. The team's work generated significant cross-government interest and international attention, culminating in my recent presentation to Swedish government colleagues seeking to replicate our approach.
